Title: Remember the Covid deaths? I do.
Date: 2026-10-19
Author: Damien Archbold
Category: News & Analysis
Slug: remember-the-covid-deaths
Summary: We thought the horrors of overflowing morgues and vulnerable poor people would wake everyone up to the need to change the way we provide healthcare. It wasn't enough.

![Refrigerated trucks serving as temporary morgues, with the Statue of Liberty visible in the background, during the COVID-19 pandemic in New York City.](/images/covid-morgue-trucks-liberty.webp)

At night in late March 2020, I arrived for yet another "Team 700" call for an intubated COVID-19 patient in cardiac arrest without enough staff or any family by her side.

Low oxygen alarms blared across the PA system. A resident physician leaned against a wall and sobbed, showing the despair and helplessness that I felt too.

After we stopped our futile resuscitation, the person's body would disappear into a refrigerated shipping container since the morgue was already full.

Covered in airway secretions with sweat soaking through my scrubs, I doffed my PPE and thought: at least after all this is over, everyone will finally understand why we need a universal health system.

Six years later, it's 2026. In the June primaries, New Yorkers sent a resounding message: we don't accept rigged political machines that sell us out to corporate donors.

The sweep by democratic socialists reaffirmed the lesson we learned from Zohran's campaign: organized people can beat organized money. "Progressive" Democrats and their liberal-sounding allies have caved to the extractive health industry for long enough.

Working class New Yorkers will join the democratic socialists for a bold demand: bring bosses down a notch and win the New York Health Act in 2027, finally taking healthcare off the bargaining table.

I heard echoes of the 7pm clapping for health workers in June's DSA primary victories. We care about our neighbors; we can and will build a better world. It's time to put healthcare back on the affordability agenda where it belongs.

*Damien Archbold is one of the two co-chairs for the NYC-DSA Healthcare Working Group and is a physician at Elmhurst Hospital in Queens.*
